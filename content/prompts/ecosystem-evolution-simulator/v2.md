<instructions>
Build a single-file, offline artificial-life ecosystem simulator. It is an educational ecology sandbox: a round petri-dish world with plant food, herbivore-like prey, and predator-like consumers governed by a real energy economy.

This must be a real running simulation, not a decorative animation. Populations should rise, fall, recover, and sometimes collapse because of the rules the user sets. The user watches the world, changes ecological parameters, presses play, and reads the resulting story in live charts.

Keep the wording and visuals safe and non-graphic. These are non-human mascot creatures in a biology-style simulation. Do not show blood, weapons, gore, realistic injury, or human harm. Use neutral labels such as "predator pressure", "capture chance", "energy transfer", "population loss", and "extinction event" where they fit.
</instructions>

<core-outcome>
The most important result is emergent predator-prey-food dynamics:

- Food availability controls prey growth.
- Prey availability controls predator recovery.
- Predator pressure controls prey crashes.
- Scarcity causes energy loss and population decline.
- Reproduction costs energy, so populations cannot grow forever.
- Food regrowth delay creates delayed feedback, which allows oscillations.

With balanced settings, prey and predator populations should oscillate out of phase for a long time. With extreme settings, the user should see believable collapses and recoveries. None of this should be scripted or drawn as fake chart data.
</core-outcome>

<energy-economy>
Every living agent has an `energy` value. Energy is the central rule of the simulation.

**Food / trees:**
- Food is represented as trees with visible green fruit or edible growth.
- Food grows over time at a configurable rate.
- Food has a configurable carrying capacity.
- Food does not reappear instantly after being eaten. Each tree or food site must have a regrowth delay.
- Eaten trees should visibly lose fruit/food and regain it only after the delay.

**Prey / herbivore mascots:**
- Prey spend energy every simulation tick for metabolism and movement.
- Prey gain energy by reaching a food tree and completing an eating action.
- Eating is not instant. Prey must pause or slow for a configurable eating time before receiving food energy.
- Prey reproduce asexually only when energy crosses a configurable threshold.
- Reproduction must cost energy. Split energy between parent and child or deduct a clear reproduction cost.
- Prey are removed from the population when energy reaches zero or when captured by a predator-like consumer.

**Predator-like consumers:**
- Predators spend energy every tick for metabolism and movement.
- Predators gain energy by catching prey and completing a short feeding/digesting action.
- Feeding is not instant. Predators must pause or slow for a configurable feeding time before gaining energy.
- Predators reproduce only when energy crosses a configurable threshold, and reproduction must cost energy.
- Predators are removed from the population when energy reaches zero.

Do not make reproduction free. Do not make food regrow instantly. Do not allow agents to ignore starvation. Those mistakes destroy the ecosystem behavior.
</energy-economy>

<headline-controls>
The user must be able to steer the ecosystem with three obvious high-level knobs:

1. **Food rate** - how quickly tree food grows and how abundant it is.
2. **Reproduction rate** - how easily prey and predators reproduce, implemented through thresholds, fertility multipliers, or both.
3. **Predator pressure** - predator effectiveness, controlled through hunt range, movement bias, capture chance, initial predator count, and/or energy gained from prey.

These knobs should have immediate visible effects. Low food should pressure prey. High food should allow prey booms. Low predator pressure should allow predators to fade out. High predator pressure should push prey down first, then predators should decline afterward if prey become scarce.
</headline-controls>

<visual-direction>
The world should feel like a small living map, not a field of random dots.

**Round 3D map:**
- Render the world as a round raised 3D/isometric petri-dish map on canvas.
- The user must be able to click/touch and drag to rotate the map.
- Clicking an agent should select it for inspection. Dragging should rotate without accidentally selecting.
- The round map must be real simulation geometry, not just a visual mask. Spawning, food placement, movement, sensing, collision response, regrowth, and boundary handling must stay inside the circular world.
- Use depth ordering, shadows, vertical offsets, terrain shading, and scale cues so the canvas world reads as a 3D map while still using vanilla JS and Canvas.

**Food visuals:**
- Food appears on trees, bushes, or small map plants with green fruit/edible nodes.
- Do not render food as random flat dots.
- Show food amount through fruit count, size, brightness, or similar visual state.

**Creature visuals:**
- Prey and predators should be cute glossy oval/bean-like mascot creatures with simple faces.
- They should look like friendly animated bubble people or jelly-bean characters, not plain balls, dots, icons, or realistic animals.
- Use clear species colors, for example blue/cyan prey and red/pink predators.
- Add small face details: eyes, blink, mouth, attention direction, or expression.
- Add life through animation: bounce, squash/stretch, lean toward targets, panic/flee motion, and small idle movement.

**Eating and feeding animation:**
- Prey must show an eating animation when using a food tree: pause, chew pulse, mouth movement, crumbs, fruit shrinking, or a short lock-on pose.
- Predators must show a feeding/digesting animation after a capture: pause, pulse, satisfied expression, small energy sparkle, or another non-graphic cue.
- These animations must reflect actual simulation states and cooldowns, not just decorative effects.
</visual-direction>

<simulation-engine>
Build a clean, deterministic simulation engine:

- Fixed-timestep simulation loop decoupled from rendering.
- Speed controls: pause, step, 1x, 5x, 20x, and turbo/max mode.
- In turbo/max mode, throttle rendering while continuing to advance simulation ticks.
- Deterministic seedable RNG so the same seed and settings reproduce the same run.
- Spatial partitioning with a grid or quadtree for nearby food/prey/predator queries. Avoid O(n^2) neighbor checks.
- Target 2,000+ agents at 60fps on the balanced preset.
- Agents must sense nearby food, prey, and predators and steer based on those signals.
- Prey should seek food while avoiding nearby predators.
- Predators should seek prey while managing energy.
- Movement should respect speed, metabolism, eating/feeding cooldowns, and the circular boundary.
</simulation-engine>

<charts-and-results>
The charts are the evidence that the simulation is alive. They must use recorded simulation history, not generated decorative curves.

Required live chart:
- Draw a scrolling population-over-time chart by hand on canvas.
- Plot food, prey, and predator counts together on one mixed graph with the same time axis.
- Do not split the population history into three separate graph bands.
- Because the counts can have different magnitudes, use normalized overlay lines, clear max/current labels, or another readable scaling method so all three lines remain visible.
- Use distinct colors and a legend.

Required extra charts / views:
- Phase-space plot: prey population on one axis, predator population on the other. A balanced run should trace a loop or spiral.
- Trait trend chart if evolution is implemented.
- Optional histograms for trait distributions.

Required summary view:
- When the user stops, a species reaches zero, or a generation cap is reached, show a run summary.
- Include peak populations, min/max/average populations, number of predator-prey oscillation cycles, and extinction/population-loss events with ticks.
- Include a one-line verdict, for example:
  - "Stable coexistence for 4,200 ticks."
  - "Prey collapsed at tick 1,850, then predators declined from food scarcity."
  - "Low food kept the ecosystem below carrying capacity."
</charts-and-results>

<evolution-stretch>
If time allows, add simple evolution and make it visible.

- Give agents heritable traits such as speed, vision, size, and metabolism.
- Mutate traits slightly on reproduction.
- Faster agents should spend more energy.
- Better sensing should have an energy or reproduction tradeoff.
- Track average trait values over time.
- Add live trait charts or histograms.
- Selection pressure should be measurable across generations, not just stored in unused fields.
</evolution-stretch>

<correctness-checks>
Before considering the app complete, verify that the simulation can demonstrate these outcomes with suitable settings:

1. Balanced coexistence where prey and predator populations oscillate out of phase for a long run.
2. High predator pressure where prey reach zero or near-zero first, followed by predator decline from lack of prey.
3. Low food where prey decline even with predators disabled or near zero.
4. Carrying capacity behavior where populations self-regulate instead of growing forever.
5. If evolution is implemented, a run where average speed or vision shifts measurably under selection pressure.
</correctness-checks>
